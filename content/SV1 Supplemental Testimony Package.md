## Summary

- [[Michael Lisenbee]] declares his role as Senior Project Manager at [[David J. Powers & Associates]] and his preparation of supplemental testimony for the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] (GOSBGF) Small Power Plant Exemption application.
- The testimony addresses noise and greenhouse gas emissions, responding to Committee Order requests in docket 20‑SPPE‑1.
- Claims that construction‐related noise will occur for less than 12 months and is consistent with San Jose’s General Plan Policy EC‑1.7.51.
- States the project’s operational noise will be below ambient levels and thus de‑minimis relative to residential receptors.
- Argues that 2016 traffic noise data remain valid as baseline, citing COVID‑19 impact on 2020 measurements.

## Key details

- **Project**: [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] (GOSBGF), a backup generating plant in San Jose, California.
- **Applicant**: [[SV1 LLC]], represented by DayZenLLC submitter role and supported by Michael Lisenbee.
- **Noise analysis**: Exhibit 4, Appendix H lists noisy activities (grading/excavation 40 days, trenching 60 days, building exterior 230 days) all under 12 months; modeled peak operational noise 47 dBA Leq during generator testing with HVAC at full load.
- **Ambient measurements**: Tables 4 & 5 in Exhibit 4 show ambient Leq levels above 55 dBA Leq, with project contribution 8–23 dBA below lowest ambient receptor readings (LT‑1, ST‑1, ST‑5).
- **Timeline**: Noise study completed December 2019; traffic data from 2015–2016 used with a 1% annual growth factor to estimate 2020 conditions.
- **Regulatory context**: California Energy Commission docket 20‑SPPE‑1; San Jose General Plan Policy EC‑1.7.51 on noisy construction.
