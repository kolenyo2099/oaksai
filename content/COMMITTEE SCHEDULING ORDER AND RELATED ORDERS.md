## Summary  
- [[California Energy Commission]] (CEC) issued a scheduling order for the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility SPPE application.  
- CEC staff will prepare an Environmental Impact Report (EIR) instead of a simpler IS/MND, citing concerns from [[CARB]] and [[BAAQMD]] about diesel‑fired backup generators.  
- The Committee set a timeline: status reports by the 10th each month, EIR scoping within 30 days before draft publication, public comment period of 45 days, and final EIR filing 20 days after comment closure.  
- Public outreach is encouraged; the Committee may hold community conferences if feasible.  
- The order was approved by Commissioner [[Karen Douglas]] and Chair [[David Hochschild]] on October 15–16, 2020.

## Key details  
- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (SPPE #20‑SPPE‑01).  
- **Applicant**: SV1, LLC, subsidiary of Equinix.  
- **Timeline**:  
  - July 13, 2020 – first Committee Conference.  
  - August 31, 2020 – second Conference setting September 23 date.  
  - Staff to file updated status reports by September 10; EIR scoping meeting within 30 days of draft publication.  
- **Environmental Review**: EIR preferred over IS/MND due to potential significant air quality and public health impacts; will include alternatives analysis.  
- **Public Participation**: Committee invites community engagement; signage at site may be considered if feasible.  
- **Contacts**: Public Advisor [[Noemí O. Gallardo]]; Hearing Officers [[Ralph Lee]], [[Linda Barrera]]; Project Manager [[Lisa Worrall]].

## Claims & attribution  
- CEC staff claims an EIR is warranted because of potential significant impacts and public concern (attributed to staff, citing CARB/BAAQMD).  
- Applicant argues EIR is unwarranted and would delay proceedings (unconfirmed by independent verification in the text).  
- Committee concludes EIR is appropriate; decision based on CEQA purpose and public interest.

Our decision is informed by the fundamental purpose of CEQA to inform the public and government decision makers of the environmental consequences of their decisions.22 To that end, an EIR is the preferred environmental review document under CEQA in this case because it will better inform the public and assist the decision makers to reach a conclusion about the potential significant effects of the proposed project. Here, Staff’s intent to conduct the more thorough analyses required under an EIR are appropriate given that CARB and BAAQMD—two agencies with expertise in air quality and public health modeling, analysis, and impacts—have expressed concerns that Staff’s analyses and modeling might not fully identify potential significant air quality and public health impacts. In addition, an EIR would help better inform the public, including residents who have expressed significant interest in the project, and allow the CEC to consider alternatives to the proposed diesel-fired backup generators. Thus, given the totality of the circumstances in this case, we agree that an EIR would best meet the purposes of CEQA, and decline Applicant’s request to direct Staff to prepare an IS/MND.
