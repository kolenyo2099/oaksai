## Summary  
- The California Energy Commission (CEC) proposed a Small Power Plant Exemption for the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] in [[San Jose]], approving it on November 24, 2021.  
- The exemption covers 39 diesel‑fired generators: 36 standby units at up to 3.25 MW each and three life‑safety units at 0.5 MW each, totaling a nameplate capacity of ~99 MW.  
- The project includes a data center with three two‑story buildings (~182,350 sq ft each) that would have a maximum electrical demand of 99 MW under worst‑case conditions.  
- The CEC determined no substantial adverse environmental or energy‑resource impacts from construction or operation, meeting the criteria for a Small Power Plant Exemption.  

## Key details  
- **Project location:** Two parcels covering ~18 acres at 123–131 Great Oaks Boulevard, bordered by Great Oaks Blvd., Via Del Oro, and San Ignacio Ave. in [[San Jose]], California.  
- **Data Center specifications:** Three buildings (~182,350 sq ft each), 15,000 sq ft office space per building; total maximum demand 99 MW (IT + cooling + ancillary).  
- **Backup Generators:**  
  - 36 standby diesel generators: peak 3.25 MW, steady‑state 2.5 MW, equipped with Miratech SCR and diesel particulate filters (Tier 4 EPA compliance).  
  - 3 life‑safety generators: 0.5 MW each for fire alarms, pumps, lighting, etc.  
  - Generators housed in six yards; two yards per building (12 units each).  
- **Regulatory framework:**  
  - Exemption under Public Resources Code §25541 and the Warren‑Alquist Act.  
  - Lead agency role under CEQA; findings: generating capacity ≤100 MW, no substantial adverse environmental or energy‑resource impacts.
