## Summary

- [[SV1, LLC]] responded to Committee questions regarding its application for a Small Power Plant Exemption (SPPE) for the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF).  
- The responses clarify that the Santa Teresa Substation and five new 21‑kV feeders are not considered part of the GOSBGF project’s environmental impacts, as they were approved prior to the facility’s construction.  
- Noise impact thresholds are addressed by referencing City of San Jose General Plan Policy EC‑1.7, stating that expected construction noise will be below significant levels and mitigated in design.  
- Ambient noise measurements show project contributions well below existing traffic noise, supporting a non‑significant noise impact assessment.  
- NOx offset requirements will be determined by the BAAQMD, with current estimates based on Tier 2 emission rates due to lack of operational data for Tier 4 SCR technology.

## Key details

- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF) – a small power plant exemption application.  
- **Location**: Carmichael, CA; project sits within the City of San Jose planning jurisdiction.  
- **Substation**: Santa Teresa Substation, approved by the CPUC and under construction; serves broader network demands, not solely GOSBGF.  
- **Feeder lines**: Five new 21‑kV distribution feeders (two per trench plus one additional), exclusively dedicated to GOSBGF, constructed over time with minimal incremental emissions.  
- **Noise thresholds**: 10 dBA significance threshold questioned; actual assessment uses a 12‑month construction period and mitigation measures to keep noise below significant levels per EC‑1.7.  
- **Ambient noise data**: Ambient levels exceeded 55 dBA Leq due to traffic; project noise remains below ambient, indicating non‑significant impact.  
- **NOx offsets**: BAAQMD will determine offset based on total emissions from maintenance/testing; current assumption equates Tier 4 SCR performance to Tier 2 rates (≈90 % NOx reduction only at full load).  
- **Emissions modeling**: Tier 4 engines expected to operate mostly below full load; thus, offsets calculated using Tier 2 emission factors.

RESPONSE TO COMMITTEE QUESTION 1 The Santa Teresa Substation has been previously approved by the CPUC and is under construction. The Santa Teresa Substation is not part of the project before the Commission as it was the subject of an earlier environmental document certified by the City of San Jose and used at the CPUC to grant PG&E the authority to construct and operate it. As described in PG&E’s responses to CEC Staff questions (TN 234392 also included as Appendix B to the FEIR), the Santa Teresa Substation is in response to customer demand in the area and has a “planned” and “ultimate” design. The “ultimate” design is capable of serving the full demand of the GOSDC at full buildout but since this substation is a network upgrade to serve future demand in the region and not built specifically for the GOSDC, ultimately PG&E would serve customers based on their actual load forecast and commitment to receive electricity at a site. SV1 has estimated its total planned development of three data centers at the site and while they are planned (and therefore subject to CEC jurisdiction) the ultimate buildout timing is not known. Therefore, SV1 has not requested full output commitments to the site and will do so as each phase of the GOSDC is built out over time. This is also proof that the Santa Teresa Substation is not part of the “whole of the action” because it is being constructed prior to GOSDC and is serving other projects as a network upgrade. RESPONSE TO COMMITTEE QUESTION 2 SV1 does not believe Staff used a 10 dBA significance threshold for construction- related noise effects but instead found that the affects would be temporary, the noisy portion of each phase of construction would be less than 12 months and SV1’s mitigation incorporated into the design of the project would reduce noise to acceptable levels.
