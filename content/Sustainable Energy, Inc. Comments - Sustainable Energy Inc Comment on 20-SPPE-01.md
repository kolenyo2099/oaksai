## Summary
- [[Sustainable Energy, Inc.]] submits comments opposing the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility exemption for installing 99 MW of diesel backup generation.  
- Proposes a 100 MWh battery system (later 300 MWh) at an estimated cost of $10 million per 100 MWh within two years, citing internal projections and Tesla’s battery performance in Australia.  
- Argues that diesel generators would be costly ($30 million for equivalent capacity) and environmentally harmful, especially during fire‑related outages.  
- Highlights potential benefits of battery backup: year‑round grid stability, vehicle‑to‑grid (V2G), ancillary services, and load shifting to reduce electricity costs.  
- Calls on the CEC to mandate battery backup instead of diesel, stressing public health and climate impacts.

## Key details
- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (20-SPPE-01) – 99 MW diesel backup for a data center.  
- **Battery proposal**: 100 MWh (later 300 MWh) battery system, $10 million per 100 MWh; total cost $30 million for 300 MWh.  
- **Diesel generator estimate**: 2.5 MW units costing $750,000 each → 40 units → $30 million.  
- **Timeline**: Battery installation projected within two years.  
- **Benefits cited**: No emissions during fire outages, lower operating costs, grid support, V2G integration, ancillary services, load shifting.

We expect to be able to do what I stated below, and if for some reason we cannot, Tesla is now expected to drive their cost of lithium batteries below $100/kWh within two years, so there are two potential sources for that time frame Re: 2. Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (20-SPPE-01): The CEC should not grant the requested exemption to Great Oaks to install 99 MW of diesel backup generation for the data center. Within 2 years a 100MWh battery system could be installed for just $10,000,000, according to our projections. It is important to consider the large stack of public benefits of requiring Great Oaks to install battery backup instead of diesel. Avoiding diesel emissions when fires may be causing outages, and blanketing the region with smoke as they did recently, is just part of it. From a cost-to-install perspective, putting in and maintaining diesel generators that might only be used once a year is foolhardy. See https://www.ebay.com/i/251556496091 for an offer of 2.5 (not 3.5) MW diesel generators; they require a trailer each, and cost $750,000. 100 MW / 2.5 MW = 40. 40 x $750,000 = $30,000,000! 3.5 MW price per MW will be comparable to 2.5 MW price per MW. A battery backup system will be available for $10,000,000 per hundred MWh, or $30,000,000 for 300 MWh, within 2 years. A battery can be used year-round to stabilize the grid, and to support the transition from fossil fuel to full battery-electric vehicles with V2G, which could very easily provide the buffer the data center needs. It could even pay for itself through ancillary services, and load-shifting to least-cost times for purchasing electricity. The Tesla battery in Australia proves it. Please choose 300 MWh of pristine battery capacity, with virtually no operations cost compared to the diesel, and no pollution. You can then pair that with demand response, including V2G, which in aggregate can keep the data center going until grid service is restored. Don't allow a company to poison people and destroy the climate because it has obsolete planners making decisions!
