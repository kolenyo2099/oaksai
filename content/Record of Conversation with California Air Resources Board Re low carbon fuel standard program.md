## Summary  
- On **July 17, 2020**, staff from the [[California Energy Commission]] (CEC) Air Quality Unit and the [[California Air Resources Board]] (CARB) discussed using renewable diesel in backup generators at data centers.  
- CEC staff queried whether stationary source fuel users could earn low‑carbon fuel standard (LCFS) credits; CARB confirmed that only transportation fuels are currently eligible.  
- CARB indicated there is no proposal to expand LCFS eligibility to stationary engines and suggested a separate program would be needed for such sources.  

## Key details  
- **Meeting date:** 7 / 17 / 2020 (phone)  
- **Participants:** Gerry Bemis (CEC Air Quality Unit), Rachel Connors, Arpit Soni (CARB LCFS Program staff).  
- **Topic:** Eligibility of stationary source fuel users—specifically backup diesel generators at data centers—to receive LCFS credits when using renewable diesel.  
- **Outcome:** Confirmation that only transportation fuels qualify for LCFS; no current or imminent expansion to stationary sources.  

## Claims & attribution  
- **Claim by CARB staff:** Only transportation fuels are eligible for LCFS credits.  
- **Claim by CARB staff (unverified):** No proposal exists to broaden LCFS eligibility to stationary engine uses; a separate program would be required.  
- These statements are taken directly from the conversation record and are not independently verified in external sources.
