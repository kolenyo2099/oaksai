## Summary  
- The document is a Section 5 alternatives analysis for the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] (GOSDC) project in San Jose, filed by [[DayZenLLC]] on 25 Aug 2020.  
- Alternatives considered include “no project”, reduced development, reduced backup electric generation, and various backup technology options.  
- The “No Project” alternative was rejected because it would fail to meet the project’s core objectives of providing state‑of‑the‑art data center capacity and reliable backup power.  
- A “Reduced Development” scenario would limit construction impacts but could not satisfy projected customer demand over time.  
- Backup generator alternatives focus on compliance with emissions controls (diesel particulate filters) and operational limitations to reduce environmental effects.

## Key details  
- **Project size**: up to 547,000 sq ft data center built in phases on an 18‑acre site.  
- **Backup generation**: original Special Use Permit (SUP) approved 25 Jan 2017 included 21 × 3‑MW generators; the revised plan proposes additional units but maintains diesel particulate filters and limits maintenance/testing hours.  
- **Location**: near Equinix main campus, connected to PG&E via the new Santa Teresa Substation, evaluated by the City of San Jose and California Public Utility Commission.  
- **Mitigation measures**: construction dust control, air cooling for evaporative systems, noise/vibration controls, soil contamination handling, seismic hazard assessment, and water‑use reduction through air cooling.

The purpose of including a No Project alternative is to allow decision-makers to compare the impacts of approving the project with the impacts of not approving the project. The Guidelines specifically advise that the No Project alternative is “what would be reasonably expected to occur in the foreseeable future if the project is not approved, based on current plans and consistent with available infrastructure and community services.” [Section 15126.6(e)(2)] The Guidelines emphasize that an EIR should take a practical approach, and not “…create and analyze a set of artificial assumptions that would be required to preserve the existing physical environment [Section 15126.6(e)(3)(B)].” No Project - No Development Alternative If the project were not constructed, the environmental impacts of the project would be avoided, but SV1’s basic project objectives would not be met, and the benefits associated with providing highly efficient and reliable internet and cloud services to meet the increasing demands would not be realized.
