## Summary

- [[BAAQMD]] issued a BACT guideline requiring diesel backup engines ≥1,000 bhp to meet U.S. EPA Tier 4 emissions standards.
- The determination applies to new and open permit applications with such engines deemed complete after 1/1/2020.
- BAAQMD’s New Source Review Rule (Regulation 2, Rule 2) mandates BACT review for any source increasing emissions of POC, NPOC, NOx, SO₂, PM₁₀, or CO by >10 lb per highest day.
- The guideline is based on evaluations of four emergency standby engines at the San Jose‑Santa Clara Regional Wastewater Facility (4 376 bhp each) and Sutro Tower, Inc. (1 881 bhp), plus Microsoft‑MWH Data Center in Quincy, Washington.
- Compliance limits include 0.5 NOx, 0.14 NMHC, 0.02 PM filterable, and 2.6 CO per g/bhp‑hr.

## Key details

- **Guideline Effective Date:** 1/1/2020 (for new/open permits).
- **Engine Threshold:** ≥1,000 bhp.
- **Emissions Standard Required:** U.S. EPA Tier 4.
- **Regulatory Basis:** California Health and Safety Code §42300; BAAQMD Regulation 2, Rule 2.
- **Evaluation Projects:**
  - Permit Application 27020 – San Jose‑Santa Clara Regional Wastewater Facility (four engines, each 4 376 bhp).
  - Permit Application 25115 – Sutro Tower, Inc. (1 881 bhp engine).
  - Microsoft‑MWH Data Center – Quincy, Washington (emission limits: 0.5 NOx, 0.14 NMHC, 0.02 PM filterable, 2.6 CO per g/bhp‑hr).
- **Engine Output Range:** 0.75 MW_e to 3.0 MW_e.
- **Contact Person:** Damian Breen, Senior Deputy Executive Officer (415) 749‑5041.

As part of our permitting processes, The Air District's New Source Review Rule - Regulation 2, Rule 2 (Regulation 2, Rule 2) - requires that new or modified sources of air pollutants undergo permit review for Best Available Control Technology (BACT). California Health and Safety Code Section 42300 authorizes delegation of stationary source permitting authority from the state to the local air pollution control districts, including the setting of rules and definitions.
