## Summary
- [[United States Environmental Protection Agency]] (US EPA) Office of Transportation and Air Quality received inquiries from [[California Energy Commission]] (CEC) staff about federal credits for using renewable diesel and biodiesel in backup engines at proposed data centers.  
- CEC staff, led by Gerry Bemis, contacted Paul Machiele and Robert Anderson at US EPA on July 6‑7, 2020.  
- The inquiry focused on the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility and similar sites in [[San Jose]] and [[Santa Clara]].  
- Replies from US EPA were provided on both July 6 and July 7, 2020.  

## Key details
- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (Small Power Plant Exemption TN #234348).  
- **Date of docketing**: August 18, 2020.  
- **Submitting organization**: California Energy Commission, Division of Siting, Transmission and Environmental Protection.  
- **Key personnel**: Gerry Bemis (Air Resources Supervisor I, CEC), Paul Machiele, Robert Anderson (US EPA).  
- **Subject matter**: Federal credits for renewable diesel and biodiesel versus conventional petroleum diesel in backup engines at data centers.  
- **Locations mentioned**: Cities of [[San Jose]] and [[Santa Clara]], California.  

## Claims & attribution
- **Claimed by CEC staff**: That federal credits exist for using renewable diesel/biodiesel in backup engines.  
- **Response from US EPA**: Two replies were sent (July 6 and July 7, 2020); the content of those replies is not detailed in the provided text, so the specific terms of the credits remain unverified here.
