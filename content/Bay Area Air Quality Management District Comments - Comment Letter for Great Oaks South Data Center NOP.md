## Summary  
- [[Bay Area Air Quality Management District]] (Air District) reviewed the Notice of Preparation for the Great Oaks South Data Center and requested a detailed Environmental Impact Report.  
- The project proposes three 182,350 sq‑ft data center buildings plus a backup energy generating facility with 99 MW capacity on an ~18‑acre site in San Jose, including 36 × 3.25‑MW diesel generators and 3 × 0.5‑MW diesel generators.  
- Air District highlighted concerns about diesel‑fueled generators near residential areas and urged the California Energy Commission (CEC) to require cleaner technologies such as solar‑battery power or fuel cells, or at least relocate generators away from receptors.  
- The letter calls for comprehensive GHG impact analysis, health risk assessment of toxic air contaminants (TAC) and PM2.5, evaluation of non‑testing/non‑maintenance generator operations, and a robust alternatives analysis aligned with CA climate goals.  
- Air District recommends mitigation measures including diesel‑free strategies, renewable energy procurement, energy efficiency targets (PUE ≤ 1.2), electric vehicle support, and carbon offsets.  

## Key details  
- **Project scope**: 18 acres, San Jose; three data center buildings (182,350 sq‑ft each); backup generator plant – 99 MW total capacity.  
- **Generator configuration**: 36 units × 3.25 MW diesel; 3 units × 0.5 MW diesel.  
- **Regulatory context**: CEC can grant a Small Power Plant Exemption; Air District requires an Authority to Construct and Permit to Operate for backup generators.  
- **GHG targets referenced**: CA Air Resources Board AB 32 Scoping Plan, SB 32 2030 GHG goals, state climate goals through 2050.  
- **Health risk focus**: TAC and PM2.5 exposure to sensitive populations during construction and operation; cumulative impacts assessed.  
- **Non‑testing/non‑maintenance data**: Between Sep 1 2019–Sep 30 2020, many Santa Clara County data centers operated diesel generators for non‑routine reasons, with some units running up to 400 hours in a single event.  
- **Mitigation options suggested**: Diesel‑free by ’33 initiative, renewable diesel, electric heat pump water heaters, fuel cells, solar PV + battery storage, SJCE TotalGreen program, PUE ≤ 1.2, high‑tier construction engines, traffic management, EV charging infrastructure, carbon offsets.  

## Claims & attribution  
- **Claimed**: Air District is concerned about increased emissions from diesel generators located outside each building; the project may disproportionately affect nearby residents.  
  - *Attribution*: Letter from [[Lisa Worrall]], Project Manager, Siting, Transmission and Environmental Protection Division, California Energy Commission.  
- **Claimed**: The EIR should demonstrate consistency with CA’s most recent GHG targets (AB 32, SB 32).  
  - *Attribution*: Air District staff recommendation.  
- **Claimed**: Non‑testing/non‑maintenance generator operations have been significant in other South Bay data centers; these must be included in the analysis.  
  - *Attribution*: Data provided by Air District to CEC, based on preliminary information from other data centers (dates and hours cited).  
- **Unconfirmed / not independently verified**: Specific operational schedules of Great Oaks South generators; actual compliance with proposed mitigation measures.

As the lead agency, the California Energy Commission (CEC) can grant the project applicant a Small Power Plant Exemption if it finds that the proposed project would not create a substantial adverse impact on the environment or energy resources. The Project will require Air District approval of an Authority to Construct and Permit to Operate the backup diesel generators, and, as such, the Project will be required to comply with all applicable Air District regulations. The Air District has worked for many years to improve air quality and health in Santa Clara County and continues to do so today. Since the San Jose community has long been disproportionately impacted by air pollution and is identified as a priority community through our Community Air Risk Evaluation (CARE) program, the Air District is concerned about the potential for any increase in emissions that could result from the Project. Specifically, the Air District is concerned about the diesel- fueled backup generators that would be located outside of each building. Because residences are located near the Project site, the Air District highly recommends that the CEC consider requiring the project proponent to use the cleanest available technologies such as solar battery power or fuel cells or, at a minimum, relocating the generators further away from residential receptors.
