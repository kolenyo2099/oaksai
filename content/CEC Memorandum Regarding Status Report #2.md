## Summary
- [[California Energy Commission]] (CEC) issued Status Report #2 for the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] exemption project.  
- CEC requires a vehicle‑miles travelled (VMT) analysis and a Transportation Analysis (TA) report in line with CEQA, following earlier staff requests on April 7, 2020.  
- The applicant argued VMT was unnecessary; however, CEC and [[City of San Jose]] staff agreed that a TA, including an LTA, is mandatory.  
- Staff expects final reports from the applicant: a City Public Works approved TA with detailed VMT analysis, a Transportation Demand Management Plan, and an LTA by October 6, 2020.  
- CEC is preparing a Notice of Preparation of an Environmental Impact Report (EIR) pending Committee decision on the required CEQA document level.

## Key details
- **Project ID**: 20‑SPPE‑01 – Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility Small Power Plant Exemption.  
- **Timeline**:  
  - September 23, 2020 – Committee orders status reports monthly starting September 10.  
  - April 7, 2020 – Data Request Set 2 for VMT analysis.  
  - May 18, 2020 – Applicant replies that VMT is not required.  
  - October 6, 2020 – CEC staff meets with city and applicant reps to discuss outstanding transportation data.  
- **Regulatory context**:  
  - Senate Bill 743 (SB 743) mandates VMT analysis for projects in California.  
  - City of San Jose Transportation Analysis Handbook requires a TA and LTA for permits.  
- **Required deliverables from applicant**:  
  - Final TA report with VMT analysis, TDM Plan, and LTA.  
  - Construction emissions data for the 21‑kV distribution line installation and street improvements (widen sidewalks to 10 ft with tree wells).  
- **Next steps**: CEC awaits Committee decision on whether an EIR is necessary; a Notice of Preparation of an EIR will be circulated if approved.

CEC and city staff have since met to discuss the project and agreed because the CEC is preparing a new CEQA document for the project as the lead agency, a transportation analysis (TA) report consistent with the city’s Transportation Analysis Handbook1, including an evaluation of the project’s potential impacts related to VMT, is required to be in conformance with CEQA. In addition, a local transportation analysis (LTA) is required as part of the city’s permitting process. An LTA analyzes the effects of a project on local transportation, access, circulation, and related safety elements. On October 6, 2020 CEC staff met with city staff and the applicant’s representatives to discuss outstanding transportation information required to complete the VMT portion of staff’s transportation analysis. As a result, staff expects to receive a City Public Works approved final TA report including a detailed VMT analysis, a Transportation Demand Management Plan, and an LTA from the applicant. 1 City of San Jose Transportation Analysis.
