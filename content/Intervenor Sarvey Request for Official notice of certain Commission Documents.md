## Summary  
- [[Robert Sarvey]] files an intervenor request for official notice of specific Commission documents in the Great Oaks South Data Center proceeding.  
- The requested documents include the Final Commission Decision for the Laurelwood Data Center (TN 232394) and California Air Resources Board comments on air quality analysis for the Sequoia Data Center (TN 235271).  
- Sarvey argues that these documents are relevant technical evidence under Energy Commission regulations and should be admitted to inform the hearing record.  
- The State Energy Resources Conservation and Development Commission is the adjudicating body, while the California Air Resources Board provides air quality recommendations for data centers permitted by the CEC.  

## Key details  
- **Docket**: 20‑SPPE‑01 (Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility)  
- **Filing date**: 2021‑10‑05 2:17 PM  
- **Intervenor**: Robert Sarvey, located at 501 W. Grant Line Rd., Tracy, CA 95376  
- **Requested documents**:  
  - TN 232394 – Final Commission Decision for the Laurelwood Data Center (docketed 3/13/20)  
  - TN 235271 – CARB Comments on Air Quality Analysis for Sequoia Data Center (submitted 10/15/2020)  
- **Regulatory references**: Energy Commission regulations § 1212(b)(1)(C) and § 1212(c)(2); Government Code §§ 11513(c), 11515; Evidence Code § 452.  

## Claims & attribution  
- **Claimed by Sarvey**: The requested documents are technically relevant and should be officially noticed to allow their contents to inform the hearing record.  
- **Unverified assertion**: That modeling emergency operations at Great Oaks South is impossible due to speculative assumptions, as opposed to the Laurelwood decision demonstrating otherwise.

(2) California Air Resources Board Comments – CARB Comments on Air Quality Analysis TN 235271 on October 15, 2020 in the Sequoia Data center Proceeding 19-SPPE-03 Argument Section 1212(b)(1)(C) of Energy Commission regulations governing adjudicative proceedings provides that the hearing record for a proceeding shall contain “any materials or facts officially noticed.”1 And section 1212(c)(2) of Energy Commission regulations provides that for Energy Commission adjudicative proceedings a “finding may be based on any evidence in the hearing record, if the evidence is the sort of information on which 1 20 CCR § 1212 subd. (b)(1)(C). 2 responsible persons are accustomed to relying on in the conduct of serious affairs.” 2 Government Code provisions governing formal hearings for administrative adjudication provide that a “hearing need not be conducted according to technical rules relating to evidence and witnesses, except as hereinafter provided” and “[a]ny relevant evidence shall be admitted if it is the sort of evidence on which responsible persons are accustomed to rely in the conduct of serious affairs, regardless of the existence of any common law or statutory rule which might make improper the admission of the evidence over objection in civil actions.”3Additionally, these provisions specify that “[i]n reaching a decision official notice may be taken, either before or after submission of the case for decision, of any generally accepted technical or scientific matter within the agency’s special field, and of any fact which may be judicially noticed by the courts of this State.”4 Evidence Code section 452 allows courts to take judicial notice of regulations and legislative enactments issued by a public entity and official acts of state legislative, executive, and judicial departments. In the current proceeding CEC Staff is claiming that modeling emergency operations is not possible because of the speculative nature of the assumptions that must be made and the fact that the results are not meaningful. The Laurelwood decision discloses that CEC Staff can easily estimate the projects emergency emissions and the analysis of emergency operations they conducted in the Laurelwood data center proceeding demonstrated that sensitive receptors would not be impacted by emergency operations at the Laurelwood data center. In the current proceeding CEC Staff is claiming that the California Air Resources Board is not recommending that the GOSBGS conduct an analysis of emergency operations. CARB Comments on Air Quality Analysis submitted on October 15, 2020 in the Sequoia Data center Proceeding is a document provided by the Air Resources Board which makes specific recommendations on air quality analyses for data centers being permitted by the CEC of which the Great Oaks South Data Center is one of. The document explains why the trustee agency the California Air Resources Board believes emergency operations must be performed and that emergency operations are not speculative.
