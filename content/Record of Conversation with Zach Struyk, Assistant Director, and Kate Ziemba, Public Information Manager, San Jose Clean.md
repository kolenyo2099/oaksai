## Summary  
- [[Zach Struyk]], Assistant Director of [[San Jose Clean Energy]] (SJCE) confirmed that the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOS) can be powered exclusively through SJCE’s 100% Total Green program.  
- The conversation involved email exchanges with [[Tao Jiang]] of the California Energy Commission (CEC).  
- CEC is proposing a small power plant exemption (SPPE) for GOS and seeks to mitigate greenhouse‑gas impacts via the Total Green measure.  
- SJCE staff expressed willingness to provide 100% carbon‑free electricity to GOS and offered coordination with city planning and code enforcement teams.  
- The record documents feasibility confirmation, stakeholder engagement, and offers to set up a conference call for further discussion.

## Key details  
- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOS) – small power plant exemption (SPPE) docket 20-SPPE-01.  
- **Date of conversation**: Emails dated July 16–19, 2021; meeting location not specified.  
- **Participants**:  
  - [[Zach Struyk]] – Assistant Director, SJCE  
  - [[Kate Ziemba]] – Public Information Manager, SJCE  
  - [[Tao Jiang]] – Air Resources Engineer, CEC  
  - Other CEC staff copied: Lisa De Carlo, Steven Kerr, Lisa Worrall, Joseph Hughes, Geoff Lesh, Wenjun Qian.  
- **CEC proposal** (MM GHG‑1): Require project owner to participate in SJCE’s Total Green level (100% carbon‑free electricity) for all electricity accounts associated with GOS.  
- **Feasibility outcome**: SJCE staff confirmed feasibility and willingness to supply 100% Total Green power.  
- **Additional coordination**: SJCE will connect Tao Jiang with city departments of Planning, Building, and Code Enforcement to discuss greenhouse‑gas mitigation alternatives.

## Claims & attribution  
- **Claimed by CEC** (Tao Jiang): The project owner can meet GHG mitigation requirements through participation in the Total Green program.  
  - *Attribution*: Email from Tao Jiang to SJCE staff; statement marked “feasible for SJCE to provide GOS with electricity solely through the 100% Total Green program.”  
- **Claimed by SJCE** (Zach Struyk & Kate Ziemba): It is feasible and they are willing to supply the required power.  
  - *Attribution*: Email reply from Zach Struyk, confirmed in both July 19 email and earlier correspondence.  
- **Unverified/Not independently verified**: The actual technical integration details, cost implications, and any contractual agreements between SJCE and GOS owners are not disclosed.
