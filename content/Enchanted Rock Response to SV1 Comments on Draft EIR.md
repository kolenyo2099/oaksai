## Summary

- [[Enchanted Rock]] responds to [[SV1]] comments on the draft Environmental Impact Report for the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF).  
- The company defends its emission data, citing laboratory‑verified tests of its Standard A24500 and Standard California gensets.  
- It argues that its microgrids can serve both emergency and grid support roles, offering significant air‑quality benefits over Tier 4 diesel engines.  
- Enchanted Rock provides detailed tables comparing hourly and annual emissions for its natural gas generators versus Cummins Tier 4 diesel units.  
- The response includes clarifications on the intended use of natural gas engines and their role in California’s Resource Adequacy program.

## Key details

- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF) – Small Power Plant Exemption, docket 20‑SPPE‑01.  
- **Respondent**: [[Enchanted Rock]] – developer of clean resiliency microgrids; operates >200 units in manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, and data centers.  
- **Emission tests**:  
  - Standard A24500 genset tested September 2019 at a Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory (CFR Title 40, Part 1065 & 1048).  
  - Standard California genset tested with ASTM Method D6348‑03; results shown in Table 1.  
- **Emission factors** (Standard California Genset Unit, 448 kW‑ESP):  
  - NOx: 0.022 g/bhp‑hr → 0.63 lb/day  
  - VOC: 0.006 g/bhp‑hr → 0.17 lb/day  
  - CO: 0.031 g/bhp‑hr → 0.89 lb/day  
  - PM: 0.036 g/bhp‑hr → 1.03 lb/day  
- **Comparison with Cummins Tier 4 diesel** (Miratech SCR & DPF):  
  - Hourly reductions: NOx –95%, VOC –96%, CO –99%, PM –75%.  
  - Annual emissions for a 99 MW plant: Enchanted Rock ≈ 3,335 lb/yr vs. Cummins 17,395 lb/yr; reductions 84–96% depending on pollutant.  
- **Operational context**:  
  - Microgrids support emergency use and Resource Adequacy (~60 h/year), reducing need for readiness testing (~20 h/year).  
  - Revenue from grid services offsets ownership costs, potentially equalizing or undercutting Tier 4 diesel.

## Claims & attribution

- **Enchanted Rock claims** that its emission data are laboratory‑verified and lower than those cited in the DEIR.  
- They assert their microgrids can be deployed for emergency use only, contrary to SV1’s suggestion of prior commercial marketing to Equinix.  
- The company states that grid support revenue reduces total cost of ownership, with emissions benefits ranging 75–99% hourly and 12–96% annually compared to diesel.  
- **SV1 claims**: staff used misleading emission data; Enchanted Rock’s generators are not emergency‑only; emissions from self‑generation for Resource Adequacy were omitted.  
- **Unconfirmed/Not independently verified**: specific numbers in Table 2 and Table 3 lack external corroboration within the provided text.

Enchanted Rock Response 1: The mass emissions rate data presented in Table D-9 of Appendix D in the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF) Draft Environmental Impact Report (DEIR) are the results of the emission source test data from the Enchanted Rock Standard A24500 Genset that occurred in September of 2019. The new engine (0-hour) emission source test was performed at a Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory per Code of Federal Regulation Title 40, Part 1065 and Part 1048. The testing performed was intended to document emissions limits with existing permitted installations. Over the last 18 months, however, Enchanted Rock has developed new emissions control technology designed for the California market. The new Enchanted Rock Standard California Genset Unit achieves significantly lower emissions levels that are well below the emission levels described in the GOSBGF DEIR. The California emissions package is installed in the same modular sound attenuated genset package that is quieter than typical diesel systems. Following recent emissions source testing on the Standard California Genset Unit using the ASTM Method D6348-03 test method, Enchanted Rock’s guaranteed emission levels are provided below in Table 1. What’s more, the most recent CA grid emergency and the associated Governor’s Executive Order highlight just one example of the ongoing risk of increased emergency operation in excess of 20 test hours annually. More frequent extreme weather and emergency conditions, if they continue, will result in emergency use operating hours converging with Resource Adequacy operating hours.
