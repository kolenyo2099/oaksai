## Summary
- The [[California Energy Commission]] (CEC) scheduled a remote Committee Conference on **July 13, 2020** to discuss the small power plant exemption (SPPE) application for the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]].
- The applicant is [[SV1, LLC]], a wholly‑owned subsidiary of [[Equinix, LLC]].
- The conference will review project status, address outstanding issues, and develop a schedule; it may include closed‑session deliberations per California law.
- Parties must submit Issues Identification, Status, and Proposed Schedule reports by **July 7–10, 2020** and prepare 15‑minute overviews for the meeting.
- Remote participation is via Zoom, with detailed instructions provided in the order.

## Key details
- **Project site:** 123–131 Great Oaks Boulevard, San Jose, California; ~18 acres undeveloped.  
- **Facilities:** 
  - *Great Oaks South Data Center*: three two‑story buildings (~547,050 sq ft) housing servers.  
  - *Backup Generating Facility*: 36 diesel generators (3.25 MW each) plus 12 life‑safety units (0.5 MW), totaling ~99 MW to supply the data center.
- **Regulatory context:** SPPE applies to plants generating 50–100 MW; CEC must determine no substantial adverse environmental or energy resource impacts.  
- **Committee composition (May 13, 2020):** Karen Douglas (Commissioner & Presiding Member) and David Hochschild (Chair & Associate Member).  
- **Key contacts:** Public Advisor Noemí O. Gallardo; Hearing Officers Susan Cochran & Rebecca Westmore; Project Manager Lisa Worrall.

## Claims & attribution
- The CEC claims the conference will “review current status, address outstanding issues, and develop a schedule.”  
- It is *unconfirmed* whether any decision on the SPPE will be reached at this meeting; the order states that hearings may be adjourned to a closed session.  
- The applicant’s claim of project feasibility is pending review in the upcoming hearing.

BACKGROUND AND DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED PROJECTS Small Power Plant Exemptions The CEC has the exclusive authority to consider, and ultimately approve or deny, applications for the construction and operation of thermal power plants that will generate 50 megawatts (MW) or more of electricity.4 The CEC may grant an exemption to its certification jurisdiction-- the SPPE-- for thermal powerplants generating between 50 and 100 MW. To grant an SPPE, the CEC must make three distinct determinations:  the proposed powerplant has a generating capacity up to 100 MW;  no substantial adverse impact on the environment will result from the construction or operation of the powerplant; and  no substantial adverse impact on energy resources will result from the construction or operation of the powerplant.5 The SPPE process provides a public forum allowing the applicant requesting the exemption, CEC staff (Staff), intervenors, governmental agencies, and members of the public to participate in the decision-making process. In addition to the findings above, the CEC is the “lead agency”6 under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA).7 If an SPPE is granted, responsible local land use authorities and other agencies, most notably any local air management or air pollution control district, conduct further review of the project, including any necessary environmental review under CEQA. 4 §§ 25120, 25500. 5 § 25541. 6 § 25519, subd. We refer to the statute and the Guidelines collectively as “CEQA.” 3 Proposed Project8 The proposed project site is located at 123 Great Oaks Boulevard; 127 Great Oaks Boulevard, and 131 Great Oaks Boulevard in the City of San Jose, California (Project Site). The Project Site is approximately 18 acres and is currently undeveloped. The Applicant proposes to construct and operate the Great Oaks South Data Center (Data Center), and the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (Backup Generating Facility). The Data Center would consist of three two-story buildings totaling approximately 547,050 square feet that would house computer servers in secure and environmentally controlled structures. The Backup Generating Facility would consist of 36 diesel-fired back-up generators, each with a maximum peak rating of 3.25 MW, located in six on-site generator equipment yards. The generators would provide up to 99 MW, the maximum building electrical load of the Data Center. Each building would have a total of 12 generators; two of the generators would be designated as redundant and would operate only to support building load when two of the primary generators have failed. The Backup Generating Facility would also include three 0.5 MW life safety generators, one per building, to support life safety systems, including fire suppression and other emergency operations. The Backup Generating Facility would supply power only to the Data Center. Even though the Application describes the Data Center as separate from the Backup Generating Facility, CEQA requires the CEC to consider the “whole of an action.”9 Therefore, the CEC will include both the Data Center and the Backup Generating Facility in its environmental analysis.
