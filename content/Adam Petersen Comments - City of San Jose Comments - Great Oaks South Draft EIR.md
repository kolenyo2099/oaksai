## Summary  
- [[City of San José]] issued comments on the Draft EIR for the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF)]] as part of the Great Oaks South Data Center project.  
- The comments address mitigation measures for biological, cultural, greenhouse gas, and noise impacts, requesting changes to survey schedules, mitigation language, and additional GHG reduction strategies.  
- The City emphasized its role as a Responsible Agency under CEQA and requested that all mitigation responsibilities be assigned to the Director of Planning, Building and Code Enforcement.  
- The project, filed by [[SV1, LLC]] (a wholly owned subsidiary of [[Equinix, LLC]]), proposes 36 diesel‑fired generators totaling up to 99 MW and a new substation, the [[Santa Teresa Substation]], to be supplied by [[Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E)]].  
- The comments include detailed guidance on compliance with the City’s Climate Action Plan and 2030 Greenhouse Gas Reduction Strategy.

## Key details  
- **Project scope**: Three 182,350 sq‑ft, two‑story data center buildings; 36 × 3.25 MW diesel generators plus three life‑safety units (0.5 MW each).  
- **Power infrastructure**: New PG&E substation (Santa Teresa Substation); five new 21 kV distribution feeders along Via Del Oro and Santa Teresa.  
- **Fuel storage**: Eight thousand‑gallon above‑ground tanks per generator yard.  
- **Mitigation measures requested by City**:  
  - Biological surveys to be conducted 14 days pre‑construction, split into two periods (Feb 1–Apr 30; May 1–Aug 15).  
  - Cultural resource mitigation wording amended to include a 50‑ft radius shutdown clause.  
  - Greenhouse gas mitigation to include solar, clean energy purchases, or renewable credits, with annual verification by an external auditor.  
  - Noise mitigation: limit idling of internal combustion engines, use quiet compressors, and enforce staging area placement ≥200 ft from commercial uses.  

## Claims & attribution  
- **City claims** (unverified in source): The City’s comments reflect its CEQA responsibilities and the requirement that all mitigation be overseen by the Director of Planning, Building and Code Enforcement.  
- **Project claims** (from SV1/Equinix): The GOSBGF will provide standby power only during PG&E service failures; routine operation is limited to maintenance and testing.  
- **Uncertainty**: Specific dates for mitigation implementation and compliance verification schedules are not independently confirmed in the provided text.
