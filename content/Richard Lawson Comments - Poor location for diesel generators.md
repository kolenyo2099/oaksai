## Summary
- [[Richard Lawson]] comments that the proposed location for diesel generators at Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility is too close to residential areas.  
- He cites PG&E’s unreliability last year, suggesting generators could run for days and emit pollutants.  
- Lawson argues smoke from wildfires would stagnate near hills unless high wind disperses it, increasing health risks.  
- He urges relocating the generators closer to [[101]] (State Route 101) where they are farther from residents and hilltop exhaust accumulation.  

## Key details
- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility – a small power plant exemption (TN #: 233743).  
- **Comment date**: 7/3/2020 1:48 PM (docketed 20‑SPPE‑01).  
- **Location concern**: Current site is near residential zones and hills that trap exhaust fumes.  
- **Proposed alternative**: Move generators closer to State Route 101, away from hilltop and residents.  

## Claims & attribution
- *Claim*: The current location will allow diesel generators to operate for several days due to PG&E’s unreliability.  
  - *Attribution*: Claim made by [[Richard Lawson]].  
- *Claim*: Smoke during wildfires can stagnate against hills, concentrating pollutants.  
  - *Attribution*: Lawson’s personal experience in construction.  
- *Uncertainty*: No independent verification of generator operating duration or pollution modeling is provided in the text.

DOCKETED Docket Number: 20-SPPE-01 Project Title: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility Small Power Plant Exemption TN #: 233743 Document Title: Richard Lawson Comments - Poor location for diesel generators Description: N/A Filer: System Organization: Richard Lawson Submitter Role: Public Submission Date: 7/3/2020 1:48:05 PM Docketed Date: 7/3/2020 Comment Received From: Richard Lawson Submitted On: 7/3/2020 Docket Number: 20-SPPE-01 Poor location for diesel generators I am in construction an have installed several of these back up generators for data centers. However the proposed location is extremely close to residential areas and with the unreliability that PG&E has shown last year to keep the power on; these could run for several days at a time. It is my experience that in the past during wildfires the smoke gets caught up against the hills and stagnates unless there is a high wind to push it away. If this situation occurs than the amount of pollutants from these diesel generators would accumulate and pose a threat to the health of the whole community living along the hills. Please reconsider moving this closer to 101 where it is further from residents and is not so close to the hills where the exhaust fumes can pool for long periods of time.
