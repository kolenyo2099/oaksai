## Summary  
- [[PG&E]] provided responses on August 17, 2020 to staff questions about the interconnection of the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] (GOS).  
- The “planned” Santa Teresa Substation lacks capacity for a 99 MW data center; the “ultimate” design with three 115 kV lines and twelve 21 kV feeders can support up to 135 MVA.  
- A full load study is required to determine if reconductoring or line re‑rating of the 115 kV lines is needed, estimated at 90 business days for preliminary engineering and 12–24 months for construction.  
- PG&E will manage future PSPS events by evaluating weather alerts, humidity, wind, vegetation, and real‑time observations from its Wildfire Safety Operations Center.  
- The ultimate design can interconnect the proposed GOS data center (Equinix SV11), China Mobile, and Ri Cloud.

## Key details  
- **Project title:** Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility Small Power Plant Exemption TN #234392.  
- **Interconnection layout:** Five 21 kV underground cables from Santa Teresa Substation to GOS; each cable rated ~20 MW, supporting a total of 99 MW.  
- **Santa Teresa Substation designs:**  
  - *Planned:* two 115 kV lines → one transformer → four 21 kV circuits (≈80 MVA).  
  - *Ultimate:* three 115 kV lines → three transformers → twelve 21 kV circuits (≈135 MVA).  
- **Transformer ratings:** 45 MVA each; two of the three can supply full load if one is offline.  
- **Load study timeline:** 90 business days for Preliminary Engineering Study (PES); reconductoring may take 12–24 months plus environmental review (6–8 months).  
- **Reliability measures:** PG&E evaluates Red Flag warnings, humidity <20 %, wind >25 mph, vegetation conditions, and field observations before PSPS actions.

There are four circuits leaving the 21 kV bus. The “ultimate” Santa Teresa substation has three 115 kV lines connected to the 115 kV bus and three transformers between 2 the 115 kV and 21 kV bus (assume it is 21 kV). There are twelve circuits leaving the 21 kV bus. 2. Does the “planned” Santa Teresa substation allow for the interconnection of the GOS (Equinix) data center? No, the planned substation does not have sufficient capacity to serve the GOS, but the ultimate substation does. New banks and feeders will be added to Santa Teresa substation when load forecasts predict that the existing bank and/or feeders will be above normal capacity. Load forecasts include the impact of growth from both new applications for service and the added load of existing customers. 3. What project/projects trigger the need for the “ultimate” Santa Teresa substation? Load forecasts include the impact of new load from applications for service. The Great Oaks South data center would submit an application for service for one or more phases of their project and PG&E would study the impact to the system from the new load. If the load forecast shows the facilities at Santa Teresa Substation above normal capacity, a project to address the capacity need would be initiated. This project may be a reconfiguration of existing circuits, the installation of new banks and feeders, or a Request for Offer from a third-party DER provider. 4.
