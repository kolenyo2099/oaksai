## Summary
- [[Robert Sarvey]] submits comments on the Proposed Decision for the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility, arguing that the EIR fails to evaluate emergency‑mode operations.  
- He cites that both the Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD) and California Air Resources Board (CARB) have requested modeling of emergency diesel generator use, but the EIR does not incorporate these scenarios.  
- Sarvey contends that emergency operations are neither infrequent nor short‑duration, citing data from BAAQMD on Bay Area data centers, including [[Equinix Data Center SV5]] in San Jose.  
- He claims the PD’s statements about speculative modeling lack support and misrepresent the record of agency comments.  

## Key details
- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (Small Power Plant Exemption) – Docket 20‑SPPE‑01, TN 240835.  
- **Submission date**: 12/6/2021 at 12:33 PM by [[Robert Sarvey]] as an intervenor to the State Energy Resources Conservation and Development Commission (SERC).  
- **Regulatory context**: CEQA Section 15002(a) – informs decision‑makers of potential significant environmental effects; requires evaluation of all foreseeable uses, including emergency operation.  
- **Agency positions**:
  - BAAQMD: In its Notice of Preparation comments and July 6 2021 letter, urged inclusion of “various scenarios of backup power generation operations beyond routine testing and maintenance.”  
  - CARB: At the September 23 2020 committee conference, attorney Wesley Dyer stated that CARB “strongly encourages more detailed air quality impact analysis … including the likely scenario that multiple generators will be running simultaneously.”  
- **Data cited**:
  - BAAQMD data (Sep 1 2019–Sep 30 2020): ~44% of surveyed Bay Area data centers operated backup diesel generators for non‑testing/non‑maintenance purposes; some ran >50 hours in a single event.  
  - Equinix SV5: operated in emergency mode in consecutive years 2019 and 2020, indicating a 33% annual probability of emergency operation at that site.  
- **EIR status**: The current Draft EIR lacks modeling for emergency operations; Sarvey argues this omission violates CEQA’s purpose to inform about significant impacts.
