## Summary  
- The [[California Energy Commission]] (CEC) issued a Committee Proposed Decision on **November 24, 2021** for the small power plant exemption of the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]].  
- The decision recommends granting an exemption because the backup generators will have 50‑100 MW capacity and are expected to have no substantial adverse impact on environment or energy resources.  
- A public comment period ran until **December 6, 2021**, with comments required to be submitted electronically or by mail.  
- The CEC scheduled a remote hearing at its December 8, 2021 Business Meeting to adopt the decision and address any errata.  

## Key details  
- **Docket**: 20‑SPPE‑01 (Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility).  
- **Committee members**: Karen Douglas (Commissioner & Presiding Member) and David Hochschild (Chair & Associate Member).  
- **Application filing date**: March 19, 2020.  
- **Proposed capacity**: >50 MW but <100 MW.  
- **Exemption basis**: Under Public Resources Code §25541; CEC must find no substantial adverse environmental or energy‑resource impacts.  
- **Public comment window**: May 21–June 10, 2021 for draft EIR; final comments deadline December 6, 2021.  
- **Hearing date**: September 21, 2021 (public evidentiary hearing).  
- **Business meeting**: Wednesday, December 8, 2021, 10 a.m.–4 p.m., remote access via Zoom link https://energy.zoom.us/j/93869230237.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF PROPOSED DECISION The Application was submitted on March 19, 2020, to the CEC under Public Resources Code section 25541. The Warren-Alquist Act4 grants the CEC the exclusive jurisdiction to approve or deny applications for the construction and operation of thermal powerplants that will generate 50 megawatts (MW) or more of electricity.5 Section 25541 creates an exemption to this exclusive jurisdiction that is referred to as a “Small Power Plant Exemption.” To grant an exemption, the CEC must make three distinct findings:  the proposed powerplant has a generating capacity up to 100 MW;  no substantial adverse impact on the environment will result from the construction or operation of the powerplant; and  no substantial adverse impact on energy resources will result from the construction or operation of the powerplant.6 In addition, the CEC is required by law to serve as the “lead agency” under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA)7 for SPPE applications.8 Under CEQA, “project” means the “whole of an action.”9 Accordingly, the CEC evaluates the entire proposed project—the Great Oaks South Data Center, the backup generators that serve the Data Center, and other project features—under CEQA. This is collectively referred to as the “Project.” To help the Committee in reviewing the Project’s potential environmental impacts, CEC staff prepared a draft environmental impact report that was circulated to the public and to responsible and trustee agencies for a 45-day public review and comment period beginning on May 21, 2021. After the public review and comment period, CEC staff reviewed and responded to the substantive environmental comments received. CEC staff then prepared a final environmental impact report that included the comments 4 Formally known as the Warren-Alquist State Energy Resources Conservation and Development Act. See Pub. CEC staff later prepared an addendum to the final environmental impact report that contained a revised mitigation measure and a proposed mitigation monitoring or reporting program. The Committee conducted a public evidentiary hearing on September 21, 2021, to admit evidence, including the final environmental impact report and addendum, into the hearing record, to allow for testimony and cross-examination, and to receive public comment. Based on the hearing record, the Committee prepared a Proposed Decision. In its Proposed Decision, the Committee recommends that the CEC find: (1) the backup generators will have a generating capacity of more than 50 MW but less than 100 MW; (2) the construction and operation of the Project will not have any substantial adverse impact on the environment; and (3) the construction and operation of the Project will not have any substantial adverse impact on energy resources. The Committee further recommends that the CEC certify the final environmental impact report and addendum, adopt the mitigation monitoring and reporting program, and grant the small power plant exemption.
