## Summary

- The brief argues that the **Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF)**, consisting of 36 diesel generators for emergency backup to the **Great Oaks South Data Center (GOSDC)**, has not been adequately modeled under CEQA for emergency operation air‑quality impacts.  
- It cites that **Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD)** and **California Air Resources Board (CARB)** have repeatedly requested detailed modeling of emergency scenarios, including simultaneous generator operation.  
- The author contends that the existing Environmental Impact Report (EIR) fails to disclose potential significant impacts from non‑testing/non‑maintenance generator use, contrary to CEQA’s purpose.  
- Evidence is presented showing emergency generator operations are frequent and can last several hours, potentially exceeding state NO₂ standards.  
- The brief calls for inclusion of Tier 4 emission modeling and argues that the current EIR is incomplete.

## Key details

- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF) – 36 diesel generators, backup only.  
- **Purpose**: Provide emergency power to GOSDC; operates in emergency mode.  
- **Regulatory context**: CEQA § 15002(a); California Code Regs. tit. 14 §§ 15002, 15064(c).  
- **Agency comments**:
  - BAAQMD (Sept 2020) urged modeling of “various scenarios of backup power generation operations beyond routine testing and maintenance.”  
  - CARB attorney Wesley Dyer (Sept 23 2020) emphasized the need to model simultaneous generator operation and short‑term pollutant impacts.  
- **Data cited**: BAAQMD survey (Sep 2019–Sep 2020) found ~44% of surveyed data centers used generators for non‑maintenance events, with some running >400 h.  
- **Emission standards**: Project expected to meet Tier 4 diesel engine emissions; however, modeling is required to confirm impact levels.
