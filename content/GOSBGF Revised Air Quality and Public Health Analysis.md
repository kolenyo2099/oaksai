## Summary  
- [[Equinix]] updated emissions analysis to comply with new [[Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD)]] rule for emergency diesel generators >1,000 hp.  
- The facility will use EPA Tier 4 engines, reducing NOx and diesel particulate matter (DPM) but requiring a 15‑minute catalyst warm‑up period during testing.  
- Maintenance & readiness (M&R) testing is limited to 20 hrs/yr per engine; emergency operations involve 30 of the 36 [[QSK95]] engines plus all three [[QSX15]] units.  
- Emission factors for M&R use EPA Tier 2 D2‑cycle rates, while emergency periods use Tier 4 limits after warm‑up.  
- Revised stack parameters (34‑inch diameter, 872 °F) and SCR controls are included; modeled concentrations remain below applicable CAAQS and NAAQS limits.

## Key details  
- **Engine types**: 36 [[QSK95]] (≥1,000 hp, Tier 4) + 3 [[QSX15]] (<1,000 hp, Tier 2).  
- **Testing schedule**: 20 hrs/yr per engine; 30 engines run 100 hrs/yr in emergency scenarios.  
- **Emission factors**:  
  - NOx: 44.92 lbs/hr (QSK95) vs 5.98 lbs/hr (QSX15).  
  - DPM: 3.06 lbs/yr per QSK95, 0.48 lbs/yr per QSX15.  
- **Fuel use**: 161,880 gal/yr total; 4,440 gal/yr for each QSK95 during M&R.  
- **Stack specs**: 34‑inch diameter, 872 °F exit temperature, 23,910 cfm flow.  
- **Regulatory context**: Compliance with EPA Tier 4 and BAAQMD emergency generator requirements; modeled concentrations stay within CAAQS/NAAQS for NO₂, CO, SO₂, PM₁₀, PM₂․₅.

## Claims & attribution  
- **Claimed by Equinix** that using Tier 4 engines will “greatly reduce the emissions of oxides of nitrogen (NOx) and diesel particulate matter (DPM).”  
- **Equinix** asserts that the 15‑minute catalyst warm‑up is the only period where NOx control is not full, but post‑warm‑up emissions meet Tier 4 limits.  
- No independent verification provided in the document; all figures are from Equinix’s analysis.

March 2021 ATMOSPHERIC DYNAMICS , INC Meteorological & Air Quality Mode li ng 1 Revised Analyses for Air Quality and Public Health for Operations and Construction Based on the recent Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD) requirements for emergency diesel generators greater than 1,000 horsepower (hp), Equinix has updated the following analyses to reflect the new BAAQMD requirement which will now utilize Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Tier 4 engines greater than 1,000 brake horsepower (bhp). The Applicants decision to incorporate Tier 4 diesel emergency generators will result in an annual readiness and maintenance testing schedule per year per engine which would be comprised of 20 hours per year (per engine). While the use of Tier 4 diesel emergency generators will greatly reduce the emissions of oxides of nitrogen (NOx) and diesel particulate matter (DPM), during the routine testing of the engines, the catalyst used to control NOx may not be fully controlling the emissions during a short period of time (approximately 15 minutes) after the initial startup of each engine. After the initial 15-minute catalyst warmup period, the catalyst is expected to be at the temperatures needed for full NOx Tier 4 NOx control. The diesel particulate filter (DPF) has no warmup requirements and will meet the Tier 4 particulate matter limits during all minutes of each operational period. Additionally, the maintenance and readiness testing (M&R) could occur at loads in the range of 10% to 100%. Based on the range of loads during the M&R testing, it was assumed that the use of EPA Tier 2 D2 cycle emissions would still be the most representative way to quantify emissions during these M&R testing hours with the use of Tier 4 emissions rates for the emergency operation as discussed below. While the project will utilize Tier 4 engines, the continued use of Tier 2 D2 cycle emission factors for the 20 hours of readiness testing would be a conservative estimate of project impacts. For emergency operations, 30 out of the 36 QSK95 engines will be operational with all three (3) of the smaller QSX15 engines. Two emergency scenarios were used for the purposes of implementing BAAQMD policy guidance relating to emission offset screening estimates: • Scenario 1 CEC – QSK95 Engines - Maintenance and readiness testing will be limited to 20 hours/yr and will be based on EPA Tier 2 D2 cycle emissions rates at 100% load, with diesel particulate matter (DPM) limited through the use of diesel particulate filters (DPF) in order to achieve the Tier 4 controls. In accordance with BAAQMD policy guidance emergency emissions offset screening estimates are based on 30 engines with a total runtime of 100 hours/yr, at 100% load, with emergency periods allocated to 3-hour blocks. Each 3-hour period will consist of a 15- minute (0.25 hr) startup or warmup period (Tier 2 D2 cycle) followed by 2.75 hours of steady state operations at the Tier 4 limits. The 1-hour startup period will be evaluated using composite weighted emissions factors based on EPA Tier 2 D2 cycle and Tier 4 limits (with DPFs). QSX15 Engines – emissions for maintenance and readiness testing and emergency operations will be based on the 20- and 100-hour runtimes using the EPA Tier 2 D2 cycled weighted emissions factors (with DPFs) as previously submitted. These engines are less than 1,000 hp and are not subject to the Tier 4 requirements. However, the engines will utilize DPF to control DPM. There are no changes to the emissions from the previous analyses. • Scenario 2 BAAQMD – QSK95 Engines - Maintenance and readiness testing will be limited to 20 hours/yr and the emissions will be based on EPA Tier 2 D2 cycle emissions rates at 100% load, with DPM limited through the use of DPFs.
