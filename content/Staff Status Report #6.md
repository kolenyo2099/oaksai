## Summary  
- [[California Energy Commission]] staff submitted Status Report #6 for the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] small power plant exemption (TN 236725) on February 10, 2021.  
- The report confirms that the project is on schedule to publish a draft Environmental Impact Report (EIR) per committee orders.  
- Staff is awaiting an updated project description including Tier 4 compliant backup generators and a complete traffic analysis with a Transportation Management Plan from the City of San Jose.  
- Draft Vehicle Miles Traveled (VMT) Analysis is under review; initial assessment requires 20 working days, plus additional time if further reviews are needed.  
- No other outstanding issues that could affect the project schedule have been identified.

## Key details  
- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility Small Power Plant Exemption (TN 236725).  
- **Docket number**: 20‑SPPE‑01.  
- **Submission date**: February 10, 2021 at 1:15 PM.  
- **Committee Conference order**: parties to file monthly status reports starting September 10, 2020.  
- **Draft VMT Analysis**: docketed on February 1, 2021 (TN 236541) by the applicant; lacks a Transportation Management Plan required by the City of San Jose Public Works.  
- **Timeline for updated project description**: estimated mid‑February 2021, to include Tier 4 compliant generators and associated impacts.  
- **Review duration**: initial 20 working days for the Draft VMT Analysis; additional 15+ days if subsequent reviews are needed.

A complete traffic analysis is an analysis that is approved by the City of San Jose Public Works and includes a detailed evaluation of the project’s potential impacts related to vehicle miles traveled, a Transportation Demand Management (TDM) Plan, and a Local Transportation Analysis. At this point, the Draft VMT Analysis includes transportation management measures but does not include a Transportation Management Plan, which will be needed for staff to complete its analysis, as both the TDM Plan and Local Transportation Analysis are required by the City of San Jose. CEC staff contacted public works staff with the city and learned that the initial review of the Draft VMT Analysis takes 20 working days and if subsequent reviews are necessary, that would add another 15 working days or more. Staff is waiting for the updated project description that includes Tier 4 compliant backup generators and description of the associated impacts, that according to the applicant’s Status Report #2, would be docketed within the next six weeks (estimated mid-February 2021). Staff continues to make progress on the EIR in those areas unaffected by the project’s conversion to Tier 4. Except for the complete traffic analysis and the Tier 4 project revisions discussed above, there are no other outstanding issues or information that could affect project schedule. Staff currently remains on track to publish the draft EIR in accordance with the Committee’s current scheduling order.
