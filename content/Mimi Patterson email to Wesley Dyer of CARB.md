## Summary  
- [[Mimi Patterson]] emails [[Wesley Dyer]] of the [[California Air Resources Board (CARB)]] regarding the Great Oaks South project.  
- The project, a three‑data‑center and small power plant site (20-SPPE-01), has faced alleged code violations and lack of public notice since 2015–17.  
- Patterson cites legal counsel [[Scott Galati]] and lobbyist [[Paul Brownell]] as pushing for a quick special use permit amendment.  
- CARB, led by Dyer, supports a full Environmental Impact Report (EIR) but is not the lead agency; the [[California Energy Commission (CEC)]] remains primary.  
- Patterson requests that the EIR be completed with due diligence and considers a new application process for the expanded generators.

## Key details  
- Project: Great Oaks South – 20‑SPPE‑01, located near South San Jose, CA.  
- Timeline: Initial application and approval in 2015–17; recent concerns surfaced early August 2020.  
- Stakeholders:  
  - [[Equinix]] (data center operator)  
  - [[Ruth and Going]] (civil engineering firm hired by Equinix)  
  - [[CEC]] (lead agency for environmental review)  
  - [[CARB]] (supporting EIR, not lead)  
  - [[Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD)]] (collaborating on review)  
- Legal/Political figures:  
  - Attorney [[Scott Galati]] (Equinix’s counsel)  
  - Lobbyist [[Paul Brownell]]  
  - Planner [[Angela Wang]] (City of San Jose Planning and Development)  
- Concerns raised: lack of public notice/signage, potential violations of city ordinance, expedited EIR/MND process.  

## Claims & attribution  
- **Claimed by Mimi Patterson** that the Equinix project was approved in 2016–17 without public outreach or signage.  
- She alleges that [[Scott Galati]] and [[Paul Brownell]] are attempting to expedite a special use permit amendment, overstating requirements.  
- Dyer states that CARB supports an EIR but CEC is the lead agency; no independent verification of claims provided in the email exchange.
