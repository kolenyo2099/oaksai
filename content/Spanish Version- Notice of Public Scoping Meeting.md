## Summary
- [[California Energy Commission]] (CEC) will hold a remote public scoping meeting on **17 Nov 2020** via Zoom to discuss the scope of an Environmental Impact Report for the [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] and [[Great Oaks South Data Center]].
- The meeting is part of a Small Power Plant Exemption (SPPE) application submitted by [[SV1, LLC]], a wholly‑owned subsidiary of [[Equinix, LLC]].
- Participants can join Zoom using ID **972 0751 9961** and password **413740**; phone dial‑ins are also available.
- Written comments on the EIR scope must be received by **24 Nov 2020 5:00 p.m.** via email or the CEC project website.
- The SPPE seeks exemption from CEC jurisdiction for a proposed 99 MW diesel‑powered backup plant at the data center site in [[San José]].

## Key details
- **Project docket:** 20‑SPPE‑01; title: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility Small Power Plant Exemption.
- **Facility layout:** Three two‑story data‑center buildings totaling 182,350 sq ft on an ~18‑acre site at 123, 127 & 131 [[Great Oaks Boulevard]].
- **Backup plant (GOSBGF):** 36 diesel generators of 3.25 MW each in six generation pads; plus three 0.50 MW “vital‑security” units; total capacity 99 MW, operating only on PG&E service failure.
- **Power connection:** New 21 kV distribution feeders to a newly built [[Santa Teresa Substation]] owned by [[PG&E]] (Pacific Gas and Electric Company).
- **Meeting logistics:** Remote via Zoom per Executive Orders N‑25‑20 & N‑29‑20; instructions provided for joining, hand‑raising (*9), muting (*6).

## Claims & attribution
- CEC claims the meeting will gather stakeholder input on EIR scope; this is stated in the public notice.
- SV1, LLC’s request for a SPPE is documented in the docket and was approved by San José city council on 1 Feb 2017 (project initially not built).
- Unverified: No independent confirmation of the exact number or capacity of generators beyond what CEC publishes; capacity figures are taken directly from the notice.
