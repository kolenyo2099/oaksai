## Summary
- [[California Energy Commission]] (CEC) staff responded to an order from the Committee regarding the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF).  
- The FEIR does **not** analyze direct impacts of the new Santa Teresa Substation but does assess the five new 21‑kV distribution feeders that will serve GOSBGF.  
- CEC explains its use of a 10 dBA threshold for construction noise, citing community reaction studies rather than an official policy.  
- Noise evaluations for both construction and operation conclude impacts remain below significant levels; mitigation includes sound walls and operational screening.  
- Cumulative impact analysis relies on a projection approach using the Envision San Jose 2040 General Plan and related FPEIR documents, following Title 14, California Code of Regulations §15130(B).

## Key details
- **Project**: Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (GOSBGF) – small power plant exemption.  
- **Feir focus**: New Santa Teresa Substation approval (City of San Jose 2017; CPUC May 6, 2021) and five new 21‑kV distribution feeders extending underground.  
- **Noise threshold**: 10 dBA increase considered significant for construction; operational noise projected below city limits.  
- **Mitigation**: Notification of residents, complaint redress process, sound walls around generator yards, HVAC units screened on rooftops.  
- **Cumulative impacts**: Projection-based analysis referencing Envision San Jose 2040 General Plan FPEIR (2016) and 4‑Year Review; focuses on biological resources, land use, noise, population/housing, transportation.

How is the 10 dBA threshold that the FEIR applies to the project’s construction- related noise effects consistent with the City of San Jose’s General Plan Policy EC- 1.7? What is the source of the 10 dBA threshold? Staff Response: Staff’s evaluation starts with an analysis of the project’s consistency with city requirements and then considers whether additional measures are necessary to ensure project impacts are less than significant under CEQA. Staff generally considers noise due to construction activities to be less than significant if the construction activity is temporary and the use of heavy equipment and noisy activities is limited to daytime hours. However, based on staff’s experience with community reaction to increases of noise due to construction, an increase of 10 dBA (which is a doubling of the dBA level) or more during the day can trigger a community reaction (e.g., a receptor hearing a 10 dBA increase due to construction noise could consider Response to Order Requesting Supplemental Information Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (20-SPPE-01) Page 3 this noise negatively) and can warrant additional measures to address impacts. The CEC has not adopted a formal threshold of significance for construction-related noise impacts, but an increase of 10 dBA is generally considered to be the starting point at which significant impacts may occur. However, whether any receptor actually perceives a 10 dBA or more increase depends on a number of factors, including whether any intervening structures would block or impede the sound waves. The exact level of noise resulting from construction is very difficult to identify because it fluctuates based on many factors over the course of a week, day, or even an hour. Therefore, performance standards (i.e., a complaint and redress process) are ultimately used as a backstop measure to address any impacts that are perceived by the community. As proposed, the project’s construction would meet the city noise requirements and, with the proposed mitigation measures, the project would not result in an increase of ambient noise at the nearest noise receptor over 10 dBA, and staff concludes the project would not result in a significant adverse impact. Updated PD NOI-1, Updated PD NOI-2, and MM NOI-1 included in the FEIR ensure: (1) the requirements of EC-1.7 are implemented; and measures will be taken to alert nearby residents of particularly noisy construction events and provide a mechanism for receiving and addressing any noise complaints. With the adoption of these measures, staff concludes the project’s noise impacts related to construction would be less than significant. 3.
