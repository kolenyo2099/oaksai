## Summary  
- [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] proposes installing diesel particulate filters (DPF) on its emergency generator engines.  
- Atmospheric Dynamics, Inc. reports that with DPF and a 20‑hour annual readiness schedule, NOx emissions are reduced to **16 t/yr** versus **97 t/yr** without DPF.  
- The study models construction and operational overlap, finding peak NO₂ concentrations of **276 µg/m³**, below the CAAQS of 339 µg/m³.  
- Worker and residential health risk assessments indicate cancer risks around **10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶** with no acute effects for DPM exposure.  
- Construction is planned in three phases, each lasting roughly 13‑15 months starting late 2020.

## Key details  
- **Project title:** [[Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility]] Small Power Plant Exemption (TN #234479).  
- **Location:** [[San Jose, California]].  
- **Engine types:** QSK95 and QSX15; 20‑hour annual maintenance, 100 % load during testing.  
- **Emissions with DPF** (Scenario 1): NOx = 81 t/yr, CO = 9.3 t/yr, VOC = 4.28 t/yr, SO₂ = 0.085 t/yr, PM₁₀ = 0.28 t/yr, CO₂e = 917 t/yr.  
- **Emissions without DPF** (Scenario 3 not used): 97 t/yr NOx, etc.  
- **Construction schedule:** Site prep 3 mo; SV12 18 mo, SV18 17 mo, SV19 17 mo; total ~350 days per phase.  
- **Health risk:** Cancer risk at residential receptor MEIR ≈ 3.7×10⁻⁵ (≈ 1 in 27,000); worker risk MEIW ≈ 7.8×10⁻⁶.  
- **Regulatory context:** Emissions below BAAQMD CEQA limits; not subject to NSR.
