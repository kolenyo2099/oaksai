## Summary
- [[California Energy Commission]] scheduled a Committee Conference for September 23, 2020 to discuss an application for a Small Power Plant Exemption (SPPE) for the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility.  
- The conference will be held remotely via Zoom in line with Executive Orders N‑25‑20 and N‑29‑20 that encourage physical distancing during COVID‑19.  
- The applicant, [[SV1, LLC]] (a subsidiary of [[Equinix, LLC]]), proposes a 18‑acre data center and backup generator site at 123–131 Great Oaks Boulevard in San Jose, California.  
- The generators would provide up to 99 MW of power for the data center’s 547,050 sq ft building complex, with 36 diesel‑fired units rated at 3.25 MW each and additional life‑safety generators.  
- The Commission will prepare an Environmental Impact Report (EIR) rather than a Mitigated Negative Declaration due to resident concerns and the scope of the project.

## Key details
- **Project site:** 123, 127, and 131 Great Oaks Boulevard, San Jose, CA – ~18 acres undeveloped.  
- **Data Center:** three two‑story buildings totaling ≈547,050 sq ft for computer servers.  
- **Backup Generators:** 36 diesel units (3.25 MW each) across six yards; total capacity ≈99 MW; plus 12 life‑safety generators (0.5 MW each).  
- **Commission staff** will file a Notice of Preparation for an EIR in August 2020, with publication no sooner than 30 days later.  
- **Committee conference:** September 23, 2020 at 1:00 p.m., remote via Zoom (Webinar ID 910 5931 3627, Passcode GOSPPE@916).  
- **Monthly status reports** required by the Committee starting September 10, 2020.  

## Claims & attribution
- The Commission **claims** that an EIR is necessary because residents have voiced concerns; this claim originates from staff’s August 7 memorandum (TN 234271).  
- The applicant **asserts** that the project will be fully contained within the 18‑acre site and that the generators will supply only the data center.  
- Uncertainty: No independent verification of resident concern levels is provided in the document; the claim is based solely on staff’s assessment.

(TN 233123.) 2 BACKGROUND The Proposed Project4 The proposed project site is located at 123 Great Oaks Boulevard, 127 Great Oaks Boulevard, and 131 Great Oaks Boulevard in the City of San Jose, California (Project Site). The Project Site is approximately 18 acres and is currently undeveloped. The Applicant proposes to construct and operate the Great Oaks South Data Center (Data Center), and the Great Oaks South Backup Generating Facility (Backup Generators) (collectively, the Project). The Data Center would consist of three two-story buildings totaling approximately 547,050 square feet that would house computer servers in secure and environmentally controlled structures. The Backup Generators would consist of 36 diesel-fired backup generators, each with a maximum peak rating of 3.25 MW, located in six on-site generator equipment yards. The generators would provide up to 99 MW, the maximum building electrical load of the Data Center. Each building would have a total of 12 generators; two of the generators would be designated as redundant and would primarily operate to support building load when two of the primary generators have failed. The Backup Generators would also include three 0.5 MW life-safety generators, one per building, to support life-safety systems, including fire suppression and other emergency operations. The Backup Generators would supply power only to the Data Center. The CEC is the “lead agency”5 under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA).6 Even though the Application describes the Data Center as separate from the Backup Generators, CEQA requires the CEC to consider the “whole of an action.”7 Therefore, the CEC will include both the Data Center and the Backup Generators in its environmental analysis.
